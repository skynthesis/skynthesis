# SKYNTHESIS Instagram Launch Playbook
**Version:** 1.0 · **Positioning:** faceless, editorial skincare discovery  
**Primary goal:** build a recognisable audience that saves, shares and returns to SKYNTHESIS, then convert interested readers to the website and newsletter.

## 1. The positioning

**Promise:** Less skincare noise. Better-informed choices.

SKYNTHESIS is not a product haul account, a repost page, or a dermatologist impersonation. It is a faceless beauty editorial desk that explains skincare decisions in a distinctive visual language: cream backgrounds, lilac typography, close-up product imagery, ingredient cards, clear caveats and concise editorial verdicts.

**Audience:** UK-first skincare shoppers, roughly 18–35, who are overwhelmed by trends, unsure what ingredients do, want to compare products before spending, or are trying to build a routine without buying everything.

**Recurring editorial series**
- **The Shelf Test:** compare two products for the same job, without pretending one is universally best.
- **Ingredient, In Plain English:** one ingredient, what it may do, what the evidence does not prove.
- **Worth the Spend?** unit price, product purpose, formula information and cheaper alternatives.
- **Routine Audit:** simplify a hypothetical routine or explain the order of steps.
- **Trend, Tested by Evidence:** explain what is known, uncertain or unsupported. Do not claim SKYNTHESIS performed laboratory or clinical tests.
- **The Brand Edit:** clearly disclosed brand-affiliation features, including Malin + Goetz.
- **Ask the Shelf:** turn audience questions into original posts and Stories.

## 2. What to learn from growth case studies

Use these as strategic clues, not guaranteed growth formulas. Agency case studies are self-reported marketing material and often combine several channels; their results should not be treated as independently verified benchmarks.

- **Faceless education:** Stan Store's September 2026 case study reports a new faceless creator-education account reaching 15,000 followers in 30 days. The transferable lesson is a narrow promise, repeatable formats, strong hooks and review of performance, not the promise of matching its follower count. https://stan.store/blog/creator-strategies-case-study/
- **Problem-led skincare content:** a 2026 PFB Vanish case study describes growth driven by search-friendly educational content and demonstrations rather than generic product promotion. For SKYNTHESIS, translate that into original explainers, routine examples and clearly sourced product comparisons. https://www.romidigital.co/work/pfb-vanish
- **Relatable, non-ad-like storytelling:** the JiYu Skin case study highlights realistic routines, creator-style storytelling and shareable content as a way to build trust. We can use hands-only product demonstrations, shelf comparisons and scenario-based explainers without showing a face. https://www.headlineideas.com/work/jiyu-skin
- **Platform guidance:** Instagram's Best Practices hub in the Professional Dashboard covers creation, engagement, reach, monetisation and guidelines. Check it monthly for account-specific recommendations. https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/
- **Originality matters:** Instagram's 2026 recommendation changes emphasise original content rather than reposting aggregators. Make our own layouts, scripts, voiceovers and edits; use retailer images only where permitted and label affiliate relationships clearly. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/

## 3. The first 30 days

Aim for a sustainable baseline, then adjust based on insights. Do not post filler just to hit a number.

**Weekly rhythm**
- 3 Reels (15–45 seconds for most explainers; use longer only when the explanation needs it).
- 2 carousels (5–8 slides; designed for saves and shares).
- Stories on 5–7 days per week: polls, question boxes, reposts of our own posts, quick comparisons and progress updates.
- 15 minutes of genuine community interaction on posting days: respond to comments, ask follow-up questions and engage thoughtfully with relevant UK skincare conversations. No spammy follow-for-follow.
- One weekly review of reach, non-follower reach, average watch time, completion/retention if available, shares, saves, profile visits, follows per post and link taps.

**Week 1 — Establish the point of view**
1. Reel: “Your skincare routine may not need another serum.”
2. Carousel: “The 3-step routine baseline: cleanse, moisturise, SPF.”
3. Reel: “Niacinamide vs vitamin C: not the same job.”
4. Carousel: “How SKYNTHESIS chooses a product: formula, evidence, fit, value.”
5. Reel: “One product category, three price points: what should you compare?”
Stories: poll on biggest skincare confusion; ask followers to submit a routine question.

**Week 2 — Build useful repeatable series**
6. Reel: “A moisturiser is not automatically better because it costs more.”
7. Carousel: “Five things to check before buying a viral skincare product.”
8. Reel: “What ‘non-comedogenic’ can and cannot tell you.”
9. Carousel: “Dark marks? Why daily sunscreen is part of the conversation.”
10. Reel: “Malin + Goetz brand edit: the seven products we’re exploring and who might consider each.” Disclose the brand affiliation in the opening frame and caption; do not present this as independent testing.
Stories: this-or-that product-purpose polls; quiz on ingredient terminology.

**Week 3 — Invite participation**
11. Reel answering a real question from comments.
12. Carousel: “Build a routine by function, not by trend.”
13. Reel: “Three skincare claims we would pause before believing.”
14. Carousel: “How to introduce one new active without changing everything at once.”
15. Reel: a hands-only shelf edit with the voiceover “what we’d buy first, and what we’d skip for now.”
Stories: anonymous question box; vote on next comparison.

**Week 4 — Double down on evidence**
16. Reel revisiting the best-performing topic with a new angle, not a duplicate upload.
17. Carousel: “The price-per-ml calculation, explained.”
18. Reel: “Retinol: who should be cautious and why the label matters.”
19. Carousel: “Our top saved answers this month.”
20. Reel: “What we changed after reading your questions.”
Stories: month recap, poll, newsletter invitation and behind-the-scenes design process.

This is a 20-feed-post starter calendar over four weeks, not a promise of growth. If production quality slips, publish fewer original pieces rather than filler.

## 4. Faceless production system

**Capture once, edit into multiple original formats.** Use hands-only footage, overhead desk shots, product textures where available, packaging close-ups, ingredient-list zooms, handwritten notes, screen recordings of SKYNTHESIS pages, and custom motion typography. Use a consistent voiceover or carefully written on-screen text; the brand should feel human even without a face.

**Reel structure**
1. 0–2 seconds: a clear problem or counterintuitive question.
2. 2–8 seconds: the useful answer or promise.
3. 8–30 seconds: 2–3 points, comparison or simple demonstration.
4. Final 2–4 seconds: one specific action, such as “Save this for your next skincare shop” or “Comment with the ingredient you want us to decode.”

Use legible captions, high contrast, accessible text size and a strong cover title. Do not rely on audio alone. Avoid watermarked reposts and do not copy another account's exact script or design.

**Carousel structure**
1. Cover: one useful, specific promise.
2. The common confusion.
3–5. Explain the key distinction with one point per slide.
6. Practical checklist or example.
7. Caveat/source note where relevant.
8. One CTA: save, share with a friend, or comment with a question.

## 6. First three post scripts

### Post 1 — Reel: “Your routine may not need another serum”
**Visuals:** hands placing several bottles aside; simple 3-step type animation; final cream/lilac brand card.

**On-screen hook:** “Before you buy another serum, check these 3 things.”

**Voiceover/caption script:** “Before adding another serum, ask: do I have a cleanser I tolerate, a moisturiser that keeps my skin comfortable, and a sunscreen I use consistently? If those basics are missing, a new active may not be the most useful next purchase. Add one product at a time, and give yourself a chance to see how your skin responds. SKYNTHESIS is here to make the choice clearer, not give you a longer shopping list.”

**CTA:** “Save this as your pre-checkout reminder.”

### Post 2 — Carousel: “The skincare label checklist”
Slide 1: “Before you buy: 6 checks for a skincare label.”
Slide 2: “1. What job is it meant to do?”
Slide 3: “2. What is the full ingredient list—not just the hero ingredient?”
Slide 4: “3. Does it duplicate something you already use?”
Slide 5: “4. What is the price per ml or gram?”
Slide 6: “5. Is there relevant evidence for the finished product, or only ingredient-level research?”
Slide 7: “6. Are the directions, warnings and seller clear?”
Slide 8: “Save this for your next skincare shop. Follow SKYNTHESIS for considered product edits.”

### Post 3 — Reel: “Malin + Goetz: the brand edit”
**Visuals:** original typographic title card, product images/packaging where permitted, one card per product function, end card with disclosure.

**On-screen hook:** “Seven Malin + Goetz products on our radar. Which actually fits your routine?”

**Voiceover/caption script:** “We love the clean, considered feel of Malin + Goetz, so we’ve put together a brand edit of seven products: cleanser, two moisturisers, SPF, a blemish treatment, a niacinamide cream and a retinol serum. This is a curated introduction, not a claim that you need the whole range or that we’ve independently tested every formula. Start with the step your routine needs, read the current ingredient list and add one new product at a time. Our full edit is on the SKYNTHESIS site.”

**Disclosure shown on screen and near the start of caption:** “Brand affiliation / affiliate links: SKYNTHESIS may earn commission from qualifying purchases at no extra cost to you.”

**CTA:** “Which category should we compare next: cleanser, moisturiser or SPF?”

## 7. Caption and discovery rules

- Lead with the actual question people search or ask: “niacinamide vs vitamin C”, “how to build a simple routine”, “what does SPF do for dark marks”.
- Use natural keywords in the first line, on-screen text, cover and caption. Use a small set of relevant hashtags only when they genuinely describe the post; hashtags are not the strategy.
- One main idea per post. Avoid vague hooks such as “You need to hear this” unless the next line names the benefit.
- Use CTAs tied to the content: save a checklist, share a comparison, comment with a question. Do not beg for engagement or use misleading bait.
- Write alt text for informative images and captions for spoken audio.
- Cite sources for scientific claims and distinguish ingredient research from evidence on the exact finished product.
- Never imply dermatologist approval, clinical testing, personal use or results unless it is documented and accurate.
- Disclose the Malin + Goetz affiliation and affiliate links clearly. Do not bury the disclosure among hashtags.

## 8. What to measure and how to adapt

Every Sunday, log each post's topic, format, hook, length, reach, non-follower reach, average watch time or retention if available, shares, saves, profile visits, follows attributed, link taps and meaningful comments.

- **High reach, low follows:** strengthen the profile promise, cover, pinned posts and CTA so a new viewer understands why to follow.
- **High saves, low reach:** make the next post a more immediately legible Reel version of the same useful idea.
- **High views, low saves/shares:** the hook may attract attention without delivering enough practical value; improve the body.
- **High profile visits, low website taps:** make the bio and landing page more specific and ensure the link works on mobile.
- **Repeated questions in comments:** turn them into a recurring series; audience questions are a content research tool.
- **One breakout post:** create 2–3 distinct follow-ups that deepen the same topic, not a copied repost.

Evaluate patterns over at least 10–15 posts before declaring a content pillar successful. Use account-specific Instagram insights rather than chasing a universal posting time or follower target.

## 9. Launch-day quality check

- [ ] Profile, bio, highlights and link are live and readable on mobile.
- [ ] Three pinned posts are published or ready.
- [ ] First 9–12 posts have a consistent visual system and distinct hooks.
- [ ] Affiliate and brand-affiliation disclosures are clear on every relevant post.
- [ ] Product names, images and destination links have been checked.
- [ ] No placeholder claims, invented reviews, unverified scores or unsupported “dermatologist-approved” language.
- [ ] A simple spreadsheet is ready to log weekly results.
- [ ] The team can answer genuine questions and correct mistakes publicly and promptly.

**Realistic expectation:** there is no reliable way to guarantee 100,000 followers. The plan is designed to increase the odds of discovery through useful original content, repeatable formats, community feedback and measured iteration—without buying followers or copying other creators.
